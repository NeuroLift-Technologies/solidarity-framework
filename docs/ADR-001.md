# ADR-001: Attenuated Capability Delegation & Composite Synthesis Checks across the Solidarity Framework

**Proposed revision:** v1.4.2 | **Status:** Draft — direction accepted, promotion pending | **Classification:** Internal

**Owner:** Joshua W. Dorsey, Sr., Managing Member, NeuroLift Technologies, LLC

Owner accepted the recommendations in the 2026-11-21 project conversation: “I'm good with your recommendations.” This records agreement with the review direction, not ADR promotion, implementation authorization, or a completed PR closure. See `OWNER-DIRECTION.md`.

This is a single ADR. The four pillars own their contract sections and the Harness has its own review workstream, but there is no per-pillar ADR split; the cross-pillar contracts (§§3.1–3.3) and the integrated acceptance gate (§5) stay in this one document. Once finalized and promoted by the owner, it is committed to the `solidarity-framework` repository as the canonical source (path and filename open, §6 O10). Until then, the Google Doc v1.3.0 Draft remains the last verified canonical text, and this draft does not replace it, approve a specification, authorize Codex implementation, or change repository, document or deployment state. Requirements below describe proposed target behavior unless explicitly identified as demonstrated evidence. Decisions recorded in v1.3.0 and Claude R1–R9 are retained (full text in §6); conflicts or underspecified details are identified rather than silently resolved.

## 1. Context and problem statement

The Solidarity Framework comprises four independently adoptable pillars. The human's Terms of Interaction (TOI) supplies human-authored interaction preferences and authority constraints; OTOI governs their operational exercise; RRT AIdvocAIte (RRTA) governs human-facing protective response; Sleepwalker Protocol governs consent and continuity. ASFDK integrates these pillars between the model and runtime. The ASFDK Harness provides enforcement at supported runtime boundaries. MCP servers, adapters and skills provide governed interaction and execution interfaces.

ADR-001 responds to multi-agent alignment degradation, delegated authority expansion, and composite information leakage. Authorized reads of two private sources do not imply permission to release the inference produced by combining them. The same failure is possible within one agent and across several agents. The prior research framing is retained from v1.3.0 (including *Aligning AI Teams* and *Authorization Propagation in Multi-Agent AI Systems: Identity Governance as Infrastructure*, arXiv:2605.05440, as cited in the earlier revisions); this revision does not independently revalidate the literature.

Claude's review reports that `honor()` accepts a tampered signed TOI, that only Pi among the reviewed harness adapters blocks before tool execution, and that PR #42's Python PolicyGateway can dispatch a private inference when caller-supplied provenance omits its source. These are reported observations at specific versions, not findings newly reproduced by this drafter. PR #42 has since been closed unmerged by the owner (2026-11-21); its probes remain as reference evidence. The opening claim of existing runtime enforcement in v1.3.0 must not be interpreted as universal pre-execution coverage. The ADR extends the existing system only where a real enforcement surface can be demonstrated.

## 2. Decision and component ownership

Retain the five contracts in §3. Their normative semantics belong to the respective pillars. Runtime integration must consume the pillar interfaces rather than duplicate their authority or policy engines.

## 3. Normative contracts (proposed target behavior)

### 3.0 Human authority resolution and tool-use posture

Preserve Claude R2–R9 as owner decisions recorded in the review: the `.toi` is human-authored; only the TOI generator writes it. OTOI is the single operational reader/resolver of TOI, using the TOI package's parsing and verification primitives; the harness consumes OTOI's effective policy, not a parallel parse. Physical write protection depends on host controls and is not established by the specification alone. A bad signature must not be represented as verified authority. The accepted diagnostic direction is a typed `resolved`, `absent` or `invalid` outcome with diagnostics; unsigned/default activation and trust-key lifecycle require explicit approval (O4).

The user tool-use posture belongs in TOI. It maps to host-native permissions (ask, approve, allow all as described in R4); the default posture is observe per R9. Exact field names, permitted values, and the mapping between observe and each native mode require specification. Posture is distinct from mandatory deployment guardrails. Resolve existing TOI tiers, validate against mandatory company guardrails, reject activation on conflict, identify the field and guardrail, and preserve the original document. Do not introduce a new TOI tier. Accepted guardrail grammar home: OTOI (O3); exact grammar remains subject to review. Adapters enforce guardrails before execution in every posture wherever technically supported; unsupported coverage must be disclosed, not presented as a successful enforcement gate.

An absent or unresolvable TOI conveys no TOI authority (R7). Native agent defaults apply subject to applicable mandatory guardrails and independently authorized scope; tell the user the reason and ask which settings apply for this session. Session clarification is not a signed TOI mutation or implicit grant. A denied/unknown authorization for a governed outbound release, or an unavailable required enforcement decision, is a separate condition from unavailable TOI. No restricted release is authorized by that fallback. Exact startup and reviewer-unavailability behavior remains open.

### 3.1 Effective Grant Intersection & Attenuated Delegation

**Policy owner: OTOI; inputs: TOI and Sleepwalker; enforcement: harness and authenticated adapters.**

Preserve v1.3.0 hybrid charter authentication: built-in and external specialist agents present a valid `agent_charter.json` signed by the authorized local host root or NLT signing key. Invalid principal: `DENY_UNVERIFIED_PRINCIPAL`. Ephemeral dynamic agents require explicit bounded human `USER_DECIDES` authorization. The source names `createFoundation` / `create_foundation` startup; this proposal retains the startup verification obligation without mandating a new charter format or duplicating crypto in ASFDK. Determine the actual canonical OTOI charter representation before choosing a wire format.

```
G_eff = G_human_task ∩ G_parent ∩ C_receiver_otoi ∩ P_sleepwalker
```

The child grant cannot exceed the parent on any capability dimension (resources, operations, recipients/destinations, expiry, depth); decreasing depth strictly contracts the overall grant envelope. Expiry cannot extend, and a zero-depth grant cannot delegate. Arbitration strategies select only from authorized options. Any expansion requires a new explicit human grant under the applicable mandatory constraints. Require authenticated parent-holder proof at child minting; the mere possession of a parent token must not authorize minting to an unrelated key. OTOI owns grant decision and validation semantics; the runtime binds them to the actual authenticated caller and receiver.

### 3.2 Provenance Attribution, Holder Binding & Replay Contract

**Policy owner: OTOI; execution reservation and provider lifecycle: harness/adapters; retention: Sleepwalker.**

Preserve intended verifier (`aud`), sender-constrained ACDT (`cnf.jkt`, SHA-256 JWK thumbprint), and RFC 9449-style proof binding (`htu`, HTTP method, `iat`, `jti`, `ath`). Bind proofs to the presented token and endpoint; reject duplicate proof identifiers in the valid replay horizon (`REPLAY_REJECTED`). A proof is not a substitute for independently authorizing the exact operation, destination and payload. Preserve `handoff_id`, `lineage_hash`, source TOI hash and verified agent attribution; separate trusted telemetry from agent self-attestation. Canonical signed-byte representation should be reconciled to RFC 8785 JCS (proposal P4); reference/production parity and wire compatibility must be demonstrated before release.

The v1.3.0 normative ACDT example fields are retained: `token_id`, `session_id`, `issuer`, `aud`, `subject_agent`, `parent_token_id`, `handoff_id`, `lineage_hash`, `toi_hash`, `sleepwalker_consent`, `cnf.jkt`, `capabilities.allowed_resources`, `capabilities.allowed_destinations`, `capabilities.denied_destinations`, `capabilities.rem_depth`, `iat`, `exp`. The exact JWT claim mapping and transport-specific proof format remain schema reconciliation tasks, not a license to invent another charter or token format.

Preserve the recorded local-first reservation choice: SQLite WAL (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`) default; optional Redis driver (`SET NX`) for distributed workers; reservation TTL bounded to parent ACDT expiry; NONE uses volatile state only. Source v1.3.0 scopes the idempotency key to `(session_id, handoff_id, operation_hash)` and releases the global gateway lock across provider I/O (reserve, release and dispatch, reacquire and finalize). A legitimate retry with a fresh proof and the same key returns the recorded result without repeating a side effect within the proven reservation domain. The harness captures the actual operation binding, and adapters enforce the provider-specific execution contract. Do not promise global exactly-once or at-most-once after loss of reservation state, a new handoff, token rotation, process restart or expiry without proof. Clarify stable logical operation identity across handoffs, in-flight expiry and NONE cleanup before implementation. Suppress automatic redispatch when the outcome is unknown.

### 3.3 Egress-Triggered Composite Synthesis Gate (CSG)

**Policy owner: OTOI; source declarations: TOI; trusted observation and gate placement: harness and adapters.**

Classify ingress at the actual trusted boundary. Preserve v1.3.0 rules: classifier bypass returns `DENY_UNCLASSIFIED_INGRESS`; registered read-only public adapters may produce `public_external` with `no_derived_egress: false`; unlabeled user pastes, local files and unregistered MCP/A2A payloads receive `unknown_taint` with `no_derived_egress: true`, allowing private response but restricting external/shared egress unless explicitly authorized by `USER_DECIDES`. Classification must reflect observed/verified origin, not caller-provided labels alone. Propagate conservative source sets through working memory, handoffs and resumed work within consent bounds. Where complete lineage cannot be established, do not infer that omitted sources are safe. A provider adapter must be bound to the registered operation and cannot be substituted by an untrusted caller.

At each outbound tool call, handoff, stream, storage write and final response, evaluate `(input_source_set, target_destination, active_toi_policy)`. After credential and revocation checks, if at least two sources contributed (or any one has `no_derived_egress`) and not all relevant derivatives are explicitly releasable to the destination, return `DENY_COMPOSITE_EGRESS` before adapter dispatch or outbound byte; apply every other independent scope restriction as well. The specific composite denial precedes generic scope denial as in v1.3.0. Ordinary single-source release is still subject to its applicable destination and scope restrictions. A legitimate private delivery is not an external release grant.

Trusted read observation and verified lineage are required; signing caller-supplied labels does not prove completeness. For a host without pre-dispatch or pre-stream interception, record the unsupported surface; do not advertise CSG enforcement there. Do not rely on the model to identify its own complete contributor set. Exact source-set granularity and any conservative overtaint tradeoff require tests and owner review.

### 3.4 Revocation, In-Flight Execution & RRTA Support Path

**OTOI:** grant revocation semantics. **RRTA:** protective response policy. **Harness/adapters:** execution stop and reconciliation. **Sleepwalker:** retention of outcome evidence.

Retain v1.3.0 RED/BLACK or manual kill-switch revocation of session and descendant ACDTs; every queued task, relay and dispatch checks immediately before dispatch. Provider acceptance and local revocation are separate events. Cancel where supported; classify `CANCELLED` only when stopped or rolled back, `COMMITTED` only on confirmed side effect (including a late commit), and `UNKNOWN` when unconfirmed. UNKNOWN is an unresolved outcome, not permanently terminal; suppress redispatch and reconcile only on reliable evidence. No guarantee of provider cancellation if unsupported.

RRTA owns the isolated direct-support contract. Following RED/BLACK revocation, use a non-delegating safe-response path with only `user:direct_support_stream`, no external tools/storage, and no task-authority restoration. Preserve v1.3.0 zero-disclosure crisis posture: calm support and crisis resources, not technical telemetry or late-commit alerts during active escalation. Defer eligible reconciliation disclosure until GREEN/YELLOW or explicit user check-in per v1.3.0; preserve Sleepwalker constraints and do not retain post-session reconciliation under NONE. A safety support path is not a blanket emergency permission to execute unrelated tasks. Confirm existing RRTA semantics before introducing code or a new handler.

### 3.5 Sleepwalker Consent & Audit Retention Contract

**Policy owner: Sleepwalker; TOI declares preferences; OTOI consumes consent constraints; each execution/storage host enforces its own sinks.**

| Consent | v1.3.0 recorded rules |
|---|---|
| NONE | No persistent prompts, outputs, inferred profiles or cross-session linkable identity. Volatile active-turn telemetry and non-linkable counters only. No post-session reconciliation records. |
| PASSIVE | Two-stage verified PII/entity scrubbing and k-anonymity/generalization; at least 10 distinct principals in a rolling 24-hour bucket; no unique timestamps, rare combinations or raw excerpts. Drop unverifiable or single-user summaries (`DENY_UNVERIFIED_ANONYMIZATION`) unless explicitly opted into the specified federated cohort, which must still meet the qualification rule. |
| ACTIVE / EXPLICIT | Encrypted-at-rest authorized workflow traces, lineage and reconciliation records, subject to user-controlled retention and deletion. Consent must be valid for the particular operation and session. |

Retain v1.3.0 consent downgrade invalidation of prior ACTIVE/EXPLICIT ACDTs and volatile cache purge; re-evaluate in-flight and queued work under the new consent. Verify real sinks including caches, replay stores, queues, traces, retries, backups and remote providers. Do not claim deletion or anonymity without evidence. The k floor and 24-hour rule are already recorded decisions (Claude O7 should not be reopened as undecided).

**Open lifecycle reconciliation (O8):** §3.2 says NONE reservation purge on turn exit; §3.5 limits operational telemetry to active turn but speaks of purge at session termination. Record per-artifact lifetimes and safe handling of pending/unknown provider outcomes; no persistent linkable data may be introduced to resolve the tension. Do not silently select a new teardown moment. The reported Worker `/process` retention and `/continuity` access concerns require deployment verification and owner-approved containment, regardless of the broader ADR schedule. The owner states the harness worker is the only Worker in use; the source trace names an `asfdk-governance` worker, and whether that is the harness worker or a separate or retired deployment is unconfirmed (§6 O6).

## 4. Consequences, rollout and evidence boundaries

This separation avoids duplicated governance engines, allows standalone pillar adoption, and makes host enforcement gaps visible. Tradeoffs include versioned cross-repo interfaces, migration effort, conservative source taint, restricted release friction and runtime interception overhead. The earlier target of under 15 ms per hop remains a target, not a demonstrated benchmark. Production claims require actual measured coverage and latency.

This remains one ADR. Each pillar owns its contract sections (§3) and has one read-only review workstream; the cross-pillar contracts and the integrated acceptance gate stay in this document. Five parallel review workstreams: TOI, OTOI, RRTA, Sleepwalker, ASFDK Harness. The harness may audit existing adapters, posture wiring and A2A boundaries immediately upon scoped review approval; new cross-pillar enforcement depends on approved interfaces. Core ASFDK remains unchanged absent demonstrated dependency integration needs. Because the harness worker is the only Worker in use, Worker verification belongs to the Harness workstream (§6 O6), not a separate Worker track. MCP servers remain separately scoped execution concerns, not additional pillars. Cross-pillar contract ownership does not authorize mutation of another repository. Review first, present spec/interface proposals, obtain owner approval, then implement with failing/regression tests and independent handback. No pushes to main, merges, deployment or unapproved architectural change.
