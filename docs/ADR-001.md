## 1. Context and problem statement

The Solidarity Framework comprises four independently adoptable pillars. The human's Terms of Interaction (TOI) is a human-authored, signed document of interaction preferences and authority constraints. OTOI is the sole operational parser, verifier and resolver of TOI, and issues the governance decisions that follow from it. RRT AIdvocAIte (RRTA) governs human-facing protective response. The Sleepwalker Protocol assures continuity: it monitors adherence to OTOI-resolved consent and purge requirements and reports drift. `nlt-sdl` (SDL) is a supporting layer that detects the user's state and issues alerts. ASFDK integrates and deploys approved pillar interfaces. The ASFDK Harness and the Cloudflare Governance MCP Worker enforce at supported runtime boundaries. A protected A2A Hub provides internal agent discovery and communication. The host or IDE shows direct governance notifications to the human.

ADR-001 responds to multi-agent alignment degradation, delegated authority expansion and composite information leakage. Authorized reads of two private sources do not imply permission to release the inference produced by combining them. The same failure is possible within one agent and across several. The research framing is retained from v1.3.0 (including *Aligning AI Teams* and *Authorization Propagation in Multi-Agent AI Systems: Identity Governance as Infrastructure*, arXiv:2605.05440, as cited in earlier revisions); this revision does not independently revalidate the literature.

Earlier revisions reported that `honor()` accepts a tampered signed TOI, that only Pi among the reviewed harness adapters blocks before tool execution, and that ASFDK PR #42's Python PolicyGateway can dispatch a private inference when caller-supplied provenance omits its source. Those remain reported observations at specific versions. PR #42 (repository `asfdk`) has since been closed unmerged, as reported by the owner; its probes are reference evidence only.

**What six read-only reviews now add.** The reviews (§4, §7) reproduced or source-confirmed, among other things: the TOI generator's output is rejected by the canonical parser; a TOI verified against its own embedded key is not thereby trusted; `honor()` resolves unsigned fixtures in both reviewed OTOI ports; RRTA gates emergency support behind Stage 1 consent and writes a plaintext per-user crisis profile; Sleepwalker permits path escape and unauthenticated cross-subject access and persists plaintext despite disabled consent; the Harness local A2A proxy dispatches for unverified callers and falls back to another agent; and SDL's detection foundation is plausible but has no authenticated alert path into the protective-response chain. None of these was observed on a live deployment, and several were observed on feature branches rather than `main` (§4.1). The opening claim of existing runtime enforcement in v1.3.0 must not be read as universal pre-execution coverage. The ADR extends the existing system only where a real enforcement surface can be demonstrated.

## 3. Normative contracts

### 3.0 Human authority resolution and tool-use posture

**Policy owner: OTOI. TOI supplies the document and the primitives. Harness and adapters consume OTOI's effective policy.**

**R2–R9 are retained [CARRIED]** (full text in §6.1). The `.toi` is human-authored, and only the TOI generator writes it. OTOI is the single operational reader and resolver, using the TOI package's parsing and verification primitives; the Harness consumes OTOI's effective policy and does not keep a parallel parse. Physical write protection depends on host controls and is not established by the specification alone. A bad signature must not be represented as verified authority.

**Three distinct properties of a TOI [READ/REPRO].** The TOI package today supplies two: structural validity (parse) and integrity against the key embedded in the document (verify). A document signed by a freshly generated, never-trusted key verifies as true, by design: TOI's own specification places key distribution and trust out of scope. The third property, **operational trusted-signer binding**, has no interface anywhere in the TOI package, and the reviewed OTOI resolver contains no signature or trusted-key gate in its resolution path. This is an unowned interface between two pillars, not an implementation oversight. Integrity against a presented key is never authority by itself, and a self-signed document is not trusted merely because its signature verifies [OWNER-derived; see O4(a) wording].

**O4(a), decided 2026-11-22 [OWNER], strict reject.** The owner-approved wording:

> OTOI shall recognize operational authority from a TOI only when its integrity and signature have been successfully verified against an established trusted signing key. If verification fails or no TOI exists, OTOI shall honor no TOI-derived authority. Native agent defaults shall apply, subject to mandatory deployment guardrails and independently authorized operational scope. The agent shall inform the human of the resolution failure and request session-specific clarification without modifying the TOI.

Scope: O4(a) only, including unsigned `.toi.default`. The fallback does not authorize bypassing a required governance decision or enforcement gate during an outage. Session clarification is not a signed TOI mutation or an implicit grant.

**Trusted-signer decision [OWNER; implementation detail OPEN].** A valid self-signature alone does not grant authority. Only a human-authorized, trusted and bound TOI signing key can establish operational TOI authority; OTOI alone parses, verifies trusted binding and resolves TOI. The private key must be protected from agents and not assumed to be NeuroLift-custodied. Bootstrap enrollment, recovery, rotation, revocation and legacy unsigned migration mechanisms remain separate specification and implementation questions, with no grant until verified. The accepted fallback for absent or invalid TOI remains R7 and the typed `absent` / `invalid` outcome. The owner-approved trust principle resolves the former O4(b) architectural choice but does not claim that any trust implementation is deployed.

**Typed outcome [OWNER direction; shape OPEN].** OTOI's resolution returns a typed `resolved`, `absent` or `invalid` outcome with diagnostics so agents and hosts can react (O5). **[REPRO]** In the reviewed OTOI ports, `honor()` resolves unsigned fixtures, and throws rather than returning a typed `absent` outcome when no document exists. The exact fields, the `posture: unresolved` status and diagnostic text remain subject to O5.

**Parser and generator integrity [TARGET].**
- A malformed, oversized or hostile TOI shall produce a typed failure and shall not crash the resolver. **[REPRO]** The reviewed TOI parser accepts a 20,000-deep document that later throws an untyped `RangeError` in canonicalization, signing or serialization. Limits and error codes are unspecified [OPEN].
- Handling of an incompatible declared major version is undecided. **[REPRO]** `$toi: "2.0.0"` and other majors currently parse as valid, and the v1.0.0 specification has no must-understand extension marker. Reject-at-parse versus preserve-and-diagnose is an owner decision [OPEN].
- Only the canonical generator writes a TOI, and it shall not emit non-canonical output. **[REPRO]** The legacy Python generator's default output validates against its own deprecated schema and is rejected by the canonical TypeScript parser, so generator → parse → sign → verify fails at its first step. Which generator is canonical is [OPEN].
- Where a cross-language signing port is built, signed bytes shall follow RFC 8785 code-unit ordering. **[READ]** The TypeScript canonicalization was reviewed as correct; a Python port that substitutes `json.dumps(sort_keys=True)` would silently diverge for astral-plane characters.

**Tool-use posture [OWNER approved vocabulary; schema and native mapping OPEN].** The user's tool-use posture belongs in TOI [CARRIED R2] and maps to each host's native permission setting (ask, approve, allow all) [CARRIED R4]. The default posture is observe [CARRIED R9]. Field names, permitted values, schema version and the mapping to each native mode require specification and approval. **[READ]** The canonical TOI schema currently contains no posture vocabulary. Posture is distinct from mandatory deployment guardrails. Resolve existing TOI tiers, validate the result against mandatory company guardrails, reject activation on conflict, identify the field and the guardrail, and preserve the original document [CARRIED R5]. Do not introduce a new TOI tier. Native posture never bypasses mandatory guardrails.

**Guardrails [OWNER approved precedence; grammar OPEN].** The accepted home of the guardrail and release grammar is OTOI; the exact grammar remains subject to review. Adapters enforce guardrails before execution in every posture wherever technically supported. Unsupported coverage shall be disclosed and shall not be presented as an enforcement gate. **[READ]** In the reviewed Harness, only the Pi `tool_call` hook is a deterministic pre-execution deny path, and only for its pattern set; the other adapters observe after the fact, are advisory or monitor-only (§3.3). Mandatory guardrail validation after resolution was not found in the reviewed OTOI entry points.

**Operative contracts must not be agent-writable [TARGET].** A governed agent shall not be able to modify the operative TOI or OTOI contracts. **[READ]** The Harness policy's sensitive-path patterns cover credentials and keys but not `.toi`, `.otoi` or `.toi.default`, so this is currently unimplemented in the reviewed Harness. Where host controls cannot prevent modification, that gap shall be disclosed. Update tools that claim TOI or OTOI enforcement shall not do so unless the harness validates; **[READ]** `asfdk_update_preferences` delegates unconditionally to the external foundation package with no harness-side validation.

**Fallback and outage [CARRIED R7; OWNER scope].** An absent or unresolvable TOI conveys no TOI authority. Native agent defaults apply subject to mandatory guardrails and independently authorized scope; tell the user the reason and ask which settings apply for this session. A denied or unknown authorization for a governed outbound release, or an unavailable required enforcement decision, is a separate condition from unavailable TOI, and no restricted release is authorized by the fallback. Exact startup and reviewer-unavailability behavior remains [OPEN]. A required decision that is unavailable is never read as permission.

**Adapter preflight and effective enforcement [OWNER contract; implementation OPEN].** Every protected operation requires an effective pre-execution denial boundary, native host hook or otherwise. Coverage is asserted per operation (`configured` ≠ `activated` ≠ `enforced`), with no production fail-open. Unsupported operations shall be identified, not represented as enforced; monitoring alone is not enforcement. The prior reviewer observed the Pi `before_agent_start` per-turn preflight disabled on the checked Harness revision [READ]; whether and how to re-enable it remains an implementation matter, not an authorization to weaken the contract.

### 3.1 Effective Grant Intersection, Attenuated Delegation and Agent Identity

**Policy owner: OTOI. Inputs: OTOI-resolved TOI policy and OTOI-resolved consent. Enforcement: Harness and authenticated adapters.**

Preserve v1.3.0 hybrid charter authentication [CARRIED]: built-in and external specialist agents present a valid `agent_charter.json` signed by the authorized local host root or NLT signing key. Invalid principal: `DENY_UNVERIFIED_PRINCIPAL`. Ephemeral dynamic agents require explicit, bounded human `USER_DECIDES` authorization. The source names `createFoundation` / `create_foundation` startup; the startup verification obligation is retained without mandating a new charter format or duplicating cryptography in ASFDK. The canonical OTOI charter representation shall be determined before any wire format is chosen.

```
G_eff = G_human_task ∩ G_parent ∩ C_receiver_otoi ∩ P_consent_otoi
```

*Note on the fourth term.* v1.3.0 and v1.4.4 wrote the fourth term as `P_sleepwalker`. Under the owner's ownership correction, consent and retention constraints are resolved by OTOI and Sleepwalker only assures adherence, so the term is renamed to avoid implying Sleepwalker as a policy input. The four-way intersection is unchanged in substance. This rename is a drafter edit made to follow the owner correction; the owner should confirm it (§6.5).

The child grant cannot exceed the parent on any capability dimension (resources, operations, recipients and destinations, expiry, depth); decreasing depth strictly contracts the overall envelope. Expiry cannot extend, and a zero-depth grant cannot delegate. Arbitration strategies select only from authorized options. Any expansion requires a new explicit human grant under the applicable mandatory constraints. Authenticated parent-holder proof is required at child minting; possession of a parent token must not authorize minting to an unrelated key. OTOI owns grant decision and validation semantics; the runtime binds them to the actual authenticated caller and receiver.

**[READ] Current state.** Reviewed OTOI charter code validates structure; strict `propagate()` compares a supplied agent ID to the charter list. No evidence was found of authenticated caller binding, bounded ephemeral grants, the four-way intersection, parent-holder proof, audience or replay checks, or revocation enforcement. The target is not implemented.

**Persistent identity versus temporary authorization [OWNER].**
- Every agent owns a unique cryptographic key pair, provisioned by the Harness and independent of the human's TOI signing keys and of OTOI's issuer signing keys. Three key domains therefore exist: human TOI signing; OTOI issuer signing under protected OTOI authority; per-agent identity.
- Stable agent identity may persist across sessions. Delegated task authorization does not: it is a separate, task-scoped credential issued by OTOI, bounded by the grant intersection above, and expires or is revoked independently of the identity.
- OTOI defines the identity, scoped-credential, rotation, revocation and cryptographic proof-of-possession contract. The Cloudflare Governance MCP Worker and Harness verify the genuine requester, the token, scope and revocation, and deny unauthenticated A2A actions. **There is no unknown-recipient fallback.**
- Normal key rotation preserves stable agent identity. Controlled rotation and revocation are approved [OWNER]; emergency recovery procedures are [OPEN]. A proposal in the Reconciliation Register, which this draft carries as a [PROPOSAL], is that a compromised key alone must not suffice to reauthorize itself.
- Exact claim names, token formats, public-key registries and algorithm choices are **not decided** and shall not be invented here. A candidate field list from earlier discussion was a proposal, not an approved schema.

**Protected Hub discovery [OWNER].** Agent Cards are discoverable only inside the protected A2A Hub. A Card carries public keys and an identity binding, never private keys. Registration, or possession of a Card, is not permission to execute work, and a verified Card proves discoverable identity, not task authorization. Public Agent Card discovery is not permitted. A Card shall not advertise governance compliance that the agent's dispatch path does not enforce [TARGET]. **[READ]** The reviewed Harness A2A proxy's registration card claims `otoi` and `toi` compliance while its dispatch path contains no governance code.

**[READ] Current local Hub and proxy.** Reviewed local Hub and proxy accept tasks from unauthenticated callers, treat caller identifiers as self-asserted, and map unknown agents by hardcoded fallback to a code-writing OpenCode agent; the Kilo plugin registers under an OpenCode identity. Services default to loopback bindings, so these are local findings, not internet exposure; the only internet-facing surface is the Worker deployment (O6). These behaviors are inconsistent with the target and are not to be carried into the Worker integration.

### 3.2 Provenance Attribution, Holder Binding & Replay Contract

**Policy owner: OTOI (credential and proof contract). Operation binding and execution reservation: Harness and adapters. Consent and purge requirements: OTOI. Adherence assurance: Sleepwalker.**

Preserve intended verifier (`aud`), sender-constrained ACDT (`cnf.jkt`, SHA-256 JWK thumbprint) and RFC 9449-style proof binding (`htu`, HTTP method, `iat`, `jti`, `ath`) [CARRIED]. Bind proofs to the presented token and endpoint; reject duplicate proof identifiers within the valid replay horizon (`REPLAY_REJECTED`). A proof is not a substitute for independently authorizing the exact operation, destination and payload. Preserve `handoff_id`, `lineage_hash`, source TOI hash and verified agent attribution; separate trusted telemetry from agent self-attestation. Reconcile the canonical signed-byte representation to RFC 8785 JCS (proposal P4); reference and production parity and wire compatibility shall be demonstrated before release. No cross-language signed-byte vector exists today.

The v1.3.0 normative ACDT example fields are retained [CARRIED]: `token_id`, `session_id`, `issuer`, `aud`, `subject_agent`, `parent_token_id`, `handoff_id`, `lineage_hash`, `toi_hash`, `sleepwalker_consent`, `cnf.jkt`, `capabilities.allowed_resources`, `capabilities.allowed_destinations`, `capabilities.denied_destinations`, `capabilities.rem_depth`, `iat`, `exp`. The exact JWT claim mapping, the per-agent proof-of-possession claims for A2A operations, and the transport-specific proof format remain schema reconciliation tasks [OPEN], not a license to invent another charter or token format. *Drafter note:* the field `sleepwalker_consent` names Sleepwalker as the source of a consent value that OTOI now resolves; whether the field is renamed or its provenance restated is part of that schema work.

**Sender proof-of-possession for A2A [OWNER architecture; format OPEN].** Authenticated A2A operations shall satisfy OTOI-defined proof-of-possession and current authorization, enforced at the Governance MCP Worker and Harness boundary. A valid proof of possession of an agent key demonstrates that the sender holds that identity; it does not authorize the operation. Replay, lineage and operation binding apply to A2A calls as to any other dispatch.

**SDL and other alert authenticity [TARGET; transport OPEN].** An alert that can influence RRTA's protective response or any revocation or purge trigger shall be authenticated to its producer, bound to its session, ordered, and resistant to replay and cross-user substitution. Invalid, out-of-order or cross-session alerts shall be rejected. The trust mechanism, and where authentication and monotonic replay suppression are enforced, is an owner decision (§6.5). **[READ]** SDL's reviewed source defines no such envelope.

**Reservation and idempotency [CARRIED].** The recorded local-first choice stands: SQLite WAL (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE`) by default; optional Redis (`SET NX`) for distributed workers; reservation TTL bounded to the parent ACDT expiry; under NONE, volatile state only. The idempotency key is scoped to `(session_id, handoff_id, operation_hash)` and the global gateway lock is released across provider I/O (reserve, release and dispatch, reacquire and finalize). A legitimate retry with a fresh proof and the same key returns the recorded result without repeating a side effect within the proven reservation domain. The Harness captures the actual operation binding; adapters enforce the provider-specific execution contract.

**Limits.** Do not promise global exactly-once or at-most-once after loss of reservation state, a new handoff, token rotation, process restart or expiry without proof. Suppress automatic redispatch when an outcome is unknown. **Under NONE** (§3.5), reservation and replay-suppression state is memory-only and is purged at the O8 boundaries; after a purge no at-most-once or restart-safe claim can be made, and any further execution of a potentially committed operation requires fresh human authorization (O8). Stable logical operation identity across handoffs, in-flight expiry, NONE cleanup, and the applicability of a local SQLite or Redis reservation store to the Worker topology are unspecified [OPEN]. **[READ]** No reservation store, cancel endpoint, UNKNOWN classification or retry suppression exists in the reviewed Harness; its task store is an in-memory map with four states.

### 3.3 Egress-Triggered Composite Synthesis Gate (CSG) and Direct Governance Notification

**Policy owner: OTOI (release decision and notification event). Source declarations: TOI via OTOI. Trusted observation, gate placement and notification carriage: Harness and adapters, with the host presenting the notification.**

Classify ingress at the actual trusted boundary [CARRIED]. Classifier bypass returns `DENY_UNCLASSIFIED_INGRESS`. Registered read-only public adapters may produce `public_external` with `no_derived_egress: false`. Unlabeled user pastes, local files and unregistered MCP or A2A payloads receive `unknown_taint` with `no_derived_egress: true`, which allows a private response but restricts external or shared egress unless explicitly authorized by `USER_DECIDES`. Classification reflects observed or verified origin, not caller-provided labels alone. Propagate conservative source sets through working memory, handoffs and resumed work within consent bounds. Where complete lineage cannot be established, do not infer that omitted sources are safe: an unknown or omitted source blocks restricted egress [TARGET]. A provider adapter is bound to the registered operation and cannot be substituted by an untrusted caller.

**Evaluation order [OWNER, composite precedence].** At each outbound tool call, handoff, stream, storage write and final response, evaluate `(input_source_set, target_destination, OTOI-resolved effective policy)`. Identity, credential and revocation are verified first. Then, if at least two sources contributed (or any one has `no_derived_egress`) and not all relevant derivatives are explicitly releasable to the destination, return `DENY_COMPOSITE_EGRESS` before adapter dispatch or any outbound byte, and apply every other independent scope restriction as well. The specific composite denial precedes generic scope denial. The composite check is not moved ahead of credential or revocation checks. Ordinary single-source release remains subject to its applicable destination and scope restrictions, and a legitimate private delivery is not an external release grant.

**Sanitized denial [CARRIED, with OWNER minimal-status rule].** The denial reason returned to the agent, and the content of any notification, shall not disclose restricted source labels, source identities or the existence of sources the receiver may not know about. The governed agent receives only the operational status necessary to comply with the decision. Gemini's v1.4.3 position that revealing combined sources is a benefit of this order is not adopted. The permitted status vocabulary is unspecified [OPEN].

**Direct governance notification [OWNER].**

> When a governance conflict requires human attention, OTOI shall emit a governance notification event intended for direct presentation to the human through the host environment, bypassing the governed agent. Notification delivery shall be facilitated through the ASFDK integration and Harness where supported. Notification contents shall respect applicable disclosure, consent and protective-response constraints. The governed agent shall receive only the operational status necessary to comply with the decision. Notification delivery, failure or dismissal shall not constitute operational authorization.

A native host or IDE pop-up is a sufficient presentation mechanism where supported. No separate messaging infrastructure and no additional pillar is prescribed. Behavior requirements [TARGET; event schema, host capability reporting, authentication and integrity, and recipient binding are OPEN]:
- The underlying decision stands unchanged whether a notification is delivered, fails to deliver, or is dismissed. Delivery state is never consulted when deciding whether an operation may proceed.
- A governance notification is minimal and non-clinical, is presented distinctly from RRTA support, and carries no restricted source labels, crisis telemetry, or deferred UNKNOWN operational state. During RED/BLACK it shall be suppressed or deferred where presenting it could defeat the protective-response and disclosure constraints of §3.4.
- Where a host cannot present a direct notification, record the unsupported surface. Do not substitute agent-mediated disclosure, and do not describe the notification as delivered.
- **[READ]** No notification producer exists in the reviewed OTOI or Harness; the Worker has no host UI, so the interface depends on a host-side client that does not yet exist. Operational error data in the reviewed OTOI conflict objects can contain raw values and source URIs and needs an explicit disclosure boundary before it is used in agent status or notifications.

**Trusted observation and gate placement [CARRIED + TARGET].** Trusted read observation and verified lineage are required; signing caller-supplied labels does not prove completeness. The model is not relied on to identify its own complete contributor set. For a host without pre-dispatch or pre-stream interception, record the unsupported surface and do not advertise CSG enforcement there. Exact source-set granularity and any conservative over-taint tradeoff require tests and owner review.

**[READ] Reviewed enforcement surfaces (Harness `5384a12`).** Pi: a deterministic `tool_call` hook for `bash`, `read`, `write` and `edit` patterns only. OpenCode plugin: post-hoc observation, and it fails open on foundation failure. Kilo plugin: content-identical to OpenCode's. OpenClaw: monitor-only; its hooks cannot cancel. Hermes: advisory tools. MCP and A2A: policy is a consultative tool the client chooses to call. No reviewed surface demonstrates CSG enforcement, and no claim of CSG coverage may be made for any of them without actual interception evidence and adapter dispatch counts. **[READ]** In the Harness, OTOI is always treated as valid-unsigned, with the OTOI chain advisory in practice. In OTOI, the context-capsule example defaults missing provenance to an empty map, which would fail open if copied.

### 3.4 Revocation, In-Flight Execution, SDL Alerts and the RRTA Support Path

**OTOI: revocation semantics and resolved requirements. SDL: state detection and alerts. RRTA: protective response. Harness and adapters: execution stop, isolation and reconciliation. Sleepwalker: assurance of adherence.**

**Revocation [CARRIED].** RED/BLACK or manual kill-switch revocation of session and descendant ACDTs; every queued task, relay and dispatch checks immediately before dispatch. Provider acceptance and local revocation are separate events. Cancel where supported; classify `CANCELLED` only when stopped or rolled back, `COMMITTED` only on confirmed side effect (including a late commit), and `UNKNOWN` when unconfirmed. UNKNOWN is an unresolved outcome, not permanently terminal; suppress redispatch and reconcile only on reliable evidence. No guarantee of provider cancellation if unsupported. Cached policy or contract state shall not outlive a revocation or purge [TARGET]. **[READ]** The Worker loads TOI and OTOI contracts once and memoizes them for the isolate lifetime, so a revocation or purge of contracts in KV would not take effect on a warm isolate; and its two KV bindings share one namespace ID, so distinct retention and purge domains cannot be enforced at the namespace level.

**SDL-1 [OWNER]: SDL detects the user's state and issues alerts.** SDL emits state observations and alerts, potentially with confidence and provenance. Its existing `recommendedMode`, `responseConstraints` and `escalateToHuman` fields are retained for backward compatibility with the published `DetectionResult` contract, but are advisory data and confer no permission, retention or protective-response authority. SDL's R11 soft-signal confidence clamp and R12 hard-distress override, and its fail-closed rules, are preserved; no new threshold or longitudinal profile is approved or implied. **[READ; reported 40/40 conformance NOT rerun]** The reviewer judged the detection wrapper and specification a plausible stateless foundation over canonical RRTA and Sleepwalker detectors, and found no TOI parser, ledger, persistent storage or secret sink in SDL source.

**RRTA-1 [OWNER].** On RED/BLACK, RRTA provides immediate crisis support and resources, **before** Stage 1 consent. This is not a general consent bypass: nonprotective actions remain subject to their ordinary consent and authority gates, and it does not restore any unrelated permission. Processing for this purpose is limited to detection and bounded support on the current interaction and is volatile (RRTA-2). **[REPRO]** In the reviewed RRTA commit, consent is checked before the self-harm branch, so a user in acute crisis without Stage 1 consent receives a generic check-in prompt, no escalation and no crisis resources; the same message after consent is granted reaches the emergency path. This is a reproduced defect against an approved decision, not an open policy question.

**RRTA-2 [OWNER].** Identifiable protective context is volatile within the active session only, with purge triggers resolved by OTOI. Support can continue from the current interaction after prior volatile context has been purged. Support continuity is thus compatible with the O8 purge. The relative ordering of purge and immediate support within one session, and the support-context lifetime, are [OPEN] (O8 detail).

**RRTA-3 [OWNER].** The isolated `user:direct_support_stream` is non-delegating and persists as a bounded direct human protective capability even when normal task authority is revoked. It confers no unrelated tools, no storage and no restoration of task authority. Following RED/BLACK revocation, RRTA uses this non-delegating safe-response path; a safety support path is not a blanket emergency permission to execute unrelated tasks. The Harness enforces the isolation where supported; if it cannot, RRTA shall not claim compliance with this section, shall record the unsupported surface, and shall fail closed with no tool use and no storage [TARGET]. **[REPRO]** None of the following exists in the reviewed RRTA commit: a `direct_support_stream` capability, a revocation state, a support-only mode, or a mapping from OTOI revocation to RRTA protective response. RRTA may hold a reference to a support state, but not an authoritative revocation or grant ledger.

**RRTA consumes OTOI-resolved requirements [OWNER ownership correction].** RRTA does not parse TOI or hold its own consent ledger. **[READ]** The reviewed RRTA contains a TOI parser and a local consent ledger (`check_consent`, `grant_consent`) and its internal `OTOIMiddleware` is a response filter, not the OTOI pillar; the naming collision should be avoided in future interface documents. How RRTA obtains an authenticated protective-state and resolved-consent signal is an interface to be approved (Annex A, I-1) [OPEN].

**Zero-disclosure crisis posture [CARRIED].** During active RED/BLACK escalation, deliver calm support and crisis resources, not technical telemetry, late-commit alerts or governance details. Defer eligible reconciliation disclosure until GREEN/YELLOW or an explicit user check-in. No post-session reconciliation is retained under NONE. **[REPRO/READ]** The reviewed RRTA returns `crisis_level` and `crisis_confidence` in its response structure and writes user-linked crisis metadata to local logs; these are disclosure and sink surfaces that must not reach user-facing text or a host-forwardable field during RED/BLACK.

**The escalation path is [OPEN, blocking].** The ADR retains that RED/BLACK triggers revocation of session and descendant credentials and OTOI-resolved purge. An SDL alert is advisory and cannot itself revoke or grant. The reviews propose several interfaces but none specifies the authenticated path by which a RED/BLACK determination reaches OTOI so that revocation and purge occur, nor who may produce it (§6.5, §6.8). A forged alert could deny a person their task authority; a missing one could leave authority in place during a crisis.

**Detection sensitivity [OPEN].** **[REPRO, baseline-sensitive]** In the reviewed RRTA probes, non-self-harm high distress was under-detected, with confidence scores placing clearly distressed messages at GREEN or YELLOW and ORANGE/RED rarely reachable from text; vaderSentiment was not installed, so a heuristic fallback was used. No threshold change is approved and no clinical validation is claimed.

**Verification gap.** **[REPRO]** The RRTA suite is red (113 collected: 110 passed, 3 failed, from a stub that omits an imported class) and `process_message`, which carries the consent, emergency and support pipeline, has no automated coverage. This is a prerequisite to any support-path work and a separately authorized change.

### 3.5 OTOI-Resolved Retention & Sleepwalker Assurance

**Policy owner: OTOI (retention eligibility and purge requirements). Assurance of adherence: Sleepwalker. Enforcement on real sinks: Harness and each execution or storage host. TOI declares preferences. RRTA and the Harness consume OTOI-resolved requirements.**

This section replaces the v1.4.4 contract titled “Sleepwalker Consent & Audit Retention.” Sleepwalker is not a retention authority.

**Framework data boundary [OWNER].** The framework shall not persist raw conversations, prompts, responses, user-linked crisis profiles or transcripts. TOI retention declarations govern the **interacting system** with which the human works; they are not a right for the governance framework to establish a personal conversation repository. A retained raw conversation is not authorized merely because the external system's preference is ACTIVE or EXPLICIT. OTOI resolves transient operational consent, allowable public verification metadata, and bounded qualified aggregate analytics. Sleepwalker verifies adherence; the Harness enforces real sinks. Permitted framework persistence is bounded to public verification keys and non-sensitive governance metadata, whose minimum operational contents are [OPEN], and to qualified non-identifying aggregates (RRTA-4).

| Consent | Rules (v1.3.0 recorded rules, as corrected for ownership and the framework data boundary) |
|---|---|
| NONE | No persistent prompts, outputs, inferred profiles or cross-session user-linkable identity. Operational state is exclusively volatile and may persist across turns within an active session (O8, below). No post-session reconciliation records. |
| PASSIVE | Two-stage verified PII and entity scrubbing and k-anonymity or generalization; at least 10 distinct principals in a rolling 24-hour bucket; no unique timestamps, rare combinations or raw excerpts. Drop unverifiable or single-user summaries (`DENY_UNVERIFIED_ANONYMIZATION`) unless explicitly opted into the specified federated cohort, which must still meet the qualification rule. Eligibility cannot be inferred from the absence of names alone. |
| ACTIVE / EXPLICIT | Applies to authorized interacting-system sinks and to bounded, encrypted-at-rest workflow traces, lineage and reconciliation records, subject to user-controlled retention and deletion; consent must be valid for the particular operation and session. It never authorizes the framework to retain raw conversations, prompts, responses or transcripts. The permissible content classes of any framework-held, user-linked ACTIVE/EXPLICIT record are [OPEN]. |

The two consent axes are distinct: Sleepwalker's intervention levels (`PASSIVE`, `LOW_PRESSURE`, `SAFETY_CHECK`, `RRTA_HANDOFF`) concern what the system may do to the user; the retention levels above concern what may be kept. **[REPRO]** The reviewed Sleepwalker has no retention type, no `NONE` or `EXPLICIT`, and the intervention enum's `PASSIVE` could be mistaken for retention `PASSIVE`, which would treat “do not prompt” as “verified anonymization.” Retention and intervention enums shall be separately namespaced [TARGET]. Retention eligibility is O7 for PASSIVE and is resolved by OTOI [CARRIED/OWNER]: the k ≥ 10, rolling 24-hour, single-user-drop floor is a recorded decision and is not reopened.

**NONE lifecycle, decided 2026-11-22 [OWNER, O8].** The owner-approved wording:

> Under NONE consent, OTOI shall resolve retention eligibility and purge requirements from the human's verified TOI. Operational state shall remain exclusively volatile and may persist across turns within an active session. OTOI shall determine the applicable purge requirements upon session termination, RED/BLACK escalation, or consent downgrade. Sleepwalker shall monitor continued adherence to these resolved requirements, detect drift, and surface violations without independently interpreting or modifying the TOI. The runtime shall enforce OTOI's resolved requirements, including authority revocation, retry suppression, cancellation where supported, and state purging. Unresolved provider outcomes shall not justify exceeding the human's retention consent. Subsequent execution of a potentially committed operation requires fresh human authorization and, where available, provider-side reconciliation.

Scope: consent takes precedence over execution continuity. No new retention exception and no guarantee of restart-safe or cross-session idempotency is established. Do not claim restart-safe idempotency under NONE. This replaces the v1.3.0 turn-exit wording for NONE reservation state. If the TOI is unsigned, absent or invalid, OTOI resolves no TOI-derived retention eligibility and the most restrictive treatment applies [TARGET; the exact default is part of O5].

**What O8 leaves open [OPEN].** Per-artifact lifetimes for caches, queued work, crisis context and local logs; exact event ordering, including purge versus immediate support (RRTA-2), and purge versus a pending notification; provider-side reconciliation mechanics; and delivery of purge evidence. Two v1.4.4 sub-questions are narrowed but not closed. First, the concern that a RED/BLACK purge destroys the record that suppresses redispatch: the O8 wording now requires fresh human authorization before executing a potentially committed operation, and RED/BLACK revocation separately removes task authority, so suppression need not depend on a retained record; this is a drafter observation, not an owner decision, and the owner should confirm it. Second, what, if anything, a user is later told about an unknown outcome after a purge, given the deferred-disclosure rule of §3.4, remains open. The consent table's v1.3.0 reference to active-turn telemetry is reconciled with O8 by the NONE row above, which states that volatile operational state, not telemetry only, may span active turns.

**Assurance and physical purge are different claims [TARGET].** Sleepwalker's monitoring verifies adherence; it does not decide eligibility. If OTOI-resolved policy is stale, missing or of an unknown version, Sleepwalker reports a policy-unavailable drift and neither falls back to parsing TOI nor acts on persistence. Verify real sinks, including caches, replay stores, queues, traces, retries, backups, remote providers and the Worker's KV. Do not claim deletion, anonymity or encryption without a receipt or evidence. Purge receipts, incomplete-purge detection and unauthorized-restore detection are required behaviors; their schema is [OPEN]. **[READ]** No purge, teardown, expiry or revoke methods exist in the reviewed Sleepwalker, and no O8 lifecycle machinery exists anywhere reviewed.

**RRTA-4 [OWNER; 2026-11-22 reaffirmed].** Non-sensitive, non-identifying learned patterns may persist for learning when OTOI authorizes them; pattern recognition is preserved. User-linked, sensitive historical profiles are prohibited. Non-identifying aggregate statistics across sessions are allowed only under OTOI-governed eligibility and the applicable verified anonymization and cohort protections (PASSIVE k ≥ 10 over 24 hours where applicable). This is not permission to retain raw individual crisis histories, and it does not authorize SDL, RRTA or any component to build longitudinal storage; whether aggregate generation lives in SDL or RRTA is a later specification choice. **[OPEN]** Whether any aggregate contribution is eligible under NONE, whose v1.3.0 row permits “non-linkable counters,” is not settled by the sources.

**Reproduced and source-level departures from this contract.**
- **[REPRO]** RRTA's `PatternAnalyzer.save_patterns()` writes an unencrypted JSON file keyed by user ID containing crisis-event counts and level distribution on every shutdown with no consent or retention check; a tracked demo artifact of this form exists. Configuration and README claims of encrypted storage, 30-day retention and “nothing stored without explicit consent” are not implemented.
- **[REPRO]** Sleepwalker's continuity store writes plaintext even with `processing_consent: false` (the setting is never read by runtime code), `privacy_mode="encrypted"` is inert, a TOI `swp.active: false` still permits both read and write, subject identifiers are caller-controlled strings used as file names, and path traversal escapes the store for both read and write. An absent TOI yields `active=True`.
- **[READ]** The Worker's configuration binds `SESSION` and `GOVERNANCE` to one KV namespace; continuity save logic writes raw user message text and raw agent response text under a 7-day TTL and injects it into later system prompts; the only expiry is the TTL, with no consent gate and no purge. This conflicts with the framework data boundary and with O8. Earlier reports that a `/process` route stores `lastMessage` for seven days and that `/continuity` is unauthenticated are **not adopted**: the later source trace finds no such explicit routes, with a bearer-token gate that fails closed ahead of all routing, and the persistence is by tool and automatic save. Whether the deployed Worker has the token secret set, its effective governance mode (`lenient` in committed configuration), and whether the traced name `asfdk-governance` is the harness worker `asfdk-governance-agent`, a separate deployment or retired, are unverified (O6).
- **Target [OWNER/TARGET].** Raw-turn persistence is removed from the target design. Doing so is a separately authorized change. Containment of any confirmed live exposure is the owner's decision; no reviewer made one.

## 4. Consequences, rollout and evidence boundaries

This separation avoids duplicated governance engines, allows standalone pillar adoption and makes host enforcement gaps visible. Tradeoffs include versioned cross-repository interfaces, migration effort, conservative source taint, restricted-release friction, runtime interception overhead, and removal of existing in-pillar parsers and ledgers. The earlier target of under 15 ms per hop remains a target and not a demonstrated benchmark. Production claims require measured coverage and latency.

**Rollout [OWNER boundary].** One ADR. Six scoped read-only review workstreams have returned: TOI, OTOI, RRTA, Sleepwalker, ASFDK Harness and the supplemental SDL workstream. “Returned” means the scoped review was delivered; it is not an approved interface, an accepted implementation, integrated tests or production assurance. Core ASFDK remains unchanged absent a demonstrated integration need and approval. There is one Cloudflare Worker target. The sequence is: owner decisions and interface approvals, then specifications, then failing tests, then separately authorized implementation, then independent handback. Cross-pillar contract ownership does not authorize mutation of another repository. No push to `main`, merge, deployment, secret rotation, deletion or unapproved architectural change is authorized by this ADR.
